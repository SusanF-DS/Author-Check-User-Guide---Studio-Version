Core Principle: An Indicator Is Not a Verdict The indicators in Author Check are not "metrics" that can be used for benchmarking, and none of them can determine misconduct by itself. A highlight indicates "worth a closer look" — not "this person has done something wrong."

Part 1: Quick Start Workflow (4 Steps)

Step 1: Find the Researcher

Log in at dimensions.ai and open Author Check from Apps in the left-hand navigation sidebar.

Three Search Methods:

  • Dimensions Researcher ID or ORCID (Most Precise): The quickest lookup without name ambiguity. Ideal when you have an ID from a manuscript submission or CV.
  • Name + Affiliation (More Precise): Best when you only have a name. Always specify institution since multiple researchers frequently share common names.
  • DOI or Dimensions Document ID (Co-authors): Resolves all co-authors listed on a published article or submitted manuscript at once.

Applying Filters & AND Logic:

Combining multiple filters (Name, Affiliation, ORCID, DOI) executes an AND search. If zero results return, remove a filter to broaden the lookup.

Step 2: Review the Summary Indicators

Work from the top overview of the researcher profile. Five indicators can turn orange when an atypical pattern is detected; publication volume is always neutral grey.

  • Publications: Peer percentile output (Grey).
  • Flagged publications: Turns orange if ≥ 1 retracted paper, notice of concern, or publication with tortured phrases.
  • Self-citations: Turns orange if author has an anomalously low self-citation rate (<80th percentile) despite qualifying across all 3 eligibility rules.
  • Unusual network flag: Turns orange if disjointed co-author clusters suggest paper mill syndicate activity.
  • Co-authors with network flags: Turns orange if ≥ 1 collaborator has an unusual network flag.
  • Co-authors with flagged publications: Turns orange ONLY if ≥ 20% of collaborators have flagged papers.

Step 3: Investigate the Details

1. Investigate Flagged Publications

The main table displays the most serious flag. Click any row to view all flags, source database (Retraction Watch, Crossref, PPS), notice date, and reason taxonomy. Distinguish between author-requested self-corrections (which count in the author's favor) and forced retractions for fraud.

2. Explore Co-Author Collaborations

Sort by Strength of Collaboration to examine whether flagged co-authors are core lab colleagues or one-time co-authors on massive consortium papers.

3. Drill into Self-Citations

Click the self-citation percentage to inspect eligible papers. Verification requires: active within the last 2 years, at least 5 eligible publications, and ≥9 references per paper. Authors with <5 papers stay neutral grey to avoid penalizing early-career scholars.

Step 4: Export Data Responsibly

Download individual tables or complete researcher dossiers as CSV or PDF via the three vertical dots menu. Note that drill-downs are capped at 500 records. Always document that findings are public metadata indicators, not legal determinations.

Part 2: The 6 Indicators Deep Dive & Thresholds

# Indicator Name The Question It Answers Highlight Threshold Peer Cohort & Rationale
1 Percentile of Publication Output How does output compare to peers in the same field and career stage? Always Grey ANZSRC Level 2 field + first publication year cohort. Contextual only. High volume alone is not misconduct.
2 Flagged Publications Has the researcher authored papers with retractions, concerns, or tortured phrases? ≥ 1 Flagged Paper Includes Retraction Watch, Crossref, and Problematic Paper Screener. A single paper warrants examining the reason notice.
3 Self-Citations Is there an anomalous absence of self-citations? < 80th %ile (typically <2%) Requires ≥5 eligible papers with ≥9 references and activity in last 2 years. Protects early career researchers. Flags authorship brokering.
4 Unusual Network Flag Does the researcher publish with disjointed clusters of unrelated co-authors? Fragmented Network Topology graph algorithm. Only 0.01% of researchers trigger this, but 50% have flagged papers (100x statistical risk of paper mills).
5 Co-authors with Network Flags Has the researcher collaborated with someone holding an Unusual Network Flag? ≥ 1 Co-Author Screens for secondary exposure to paper mill syndicates. Investigate the joint papers.
6 Co-authors with Flagged Publications What percentage of co-authors have flagged publications? ≥ 20% of Co-Authors High 20% threshold prevents false alarms, as ~30% of normal scholars have at least 1 flagged co-author across their career.

Part 3: Retraction Taxonomy & Cause Classifications

Author Check categorizes retraction notices into distinct severity levels:

  • Author Integrity: Misconduct including falsification, plagiarism, identity theft, paper mill authorship brokering, and compromised peer review.
  • Unreliable Research: Inaccurate data, calculation errors, irreproducible findings, contamination, or unverified protocols (often self-reported by authors).
  • Publisher Error: Administrative errors, duplicate publication by publisher, or accidental release of uncorrected drafts (no author fault).
  • Problematic Paper Screener (PPS): Algorithmic detection of tortured phrases (synonym-scrambled AI text) and hijacked journals.

Part 4: Responsible Role Screening Checklists

1. Screening Manuscript Authors

  • Verify if any retractions occurred in the submitted paper's research domain.
  • Distinguish between author self-corrections and forced fraud.
  • Check whether an Unusual Network Flag exists across the corresponding author or senior author.

2. Screening Potential Peer Reviewers

  • Check for low self-citation rates (anomalous reviewer identity) and active retractions.
  • Ensure co-author flags do not create hidden conflicts of interest with the submitted manuscript.

3. Screening Editorial Board Candidates

  • Examine all 6 indicators comprehensively, particularly Co-authors with Network Flags and Unusual Network topology.
  • Editorial positions carry gatekeeping authority; zero exposure to paper mill syndicates is essential.

Part 5: Core Glossary & Terminology

Term Plain-English Definition
Peer Cohort A comparison group of researchers within the same primary ANZSRC Level 2 research field who published their very first paper in the exact same year.
Tortured Phrases Unnatural scientific phrasing generated when computer-generated or translated text scrambles standard terminology to evade plagiarism detection.
Paper Mill Commercial underground operations that produce fabricated scientific manuscripts, sell authorship slots, and manipulate citations.
Expression of Concern A formal notice published by a journal editor when substantial doubts arise about a paper while a formal institutional investigation is ongoing.
ANZSRC Field of Research The Australian and New Zealand Standard Research Classification system used by Dimensions to classify researchers into fields.
